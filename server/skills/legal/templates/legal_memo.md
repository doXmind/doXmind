## Brief Answer

[Provide a concise answer to the question(s) presented. Start with a direct answer (Yes/No/Probably), then summarize the key reasoning in 2-3 sentences.]

**Example**:
"Yes, [Company] likely may terminate the Agreement for cause. Section 5.2 permits termination when the other party 'materially breaches' and fails to cure within 30 days. [Vendor]'s consistent failure to meet performance standards likely constitutes a material breach, and the required notice period has passed without cure."

## Formatting Guidelines

### Organization

- Use clear headings and subheadings
- Number paragraphs in long memos
- Use bullet points for lists
- Bold key terms and conclusions

### Writing Style

- Objective and analytical tone
- Plain language where possible
- Active voice
- Short paragraphs
- Define technical terms

### Citations

- Use proper legal citation format
- Include pinpoint citations
- Cite primary sources when possible

### Length

- Keep as concise as possible
- Typical length: 2-10 pages
- Longer analysis may require executive summary

## Sample Question Presented Formats

**Contract Issue**:
"Whether [Party A] has breached the [Agreement] by [action], thereby entitling [Party B] to [remedy]?"

**Statutory Issue**:
"Whether [conduct] violates [statute/regulation] when [key facts]?"

**Liability Issue**:
"Whether [Company] may be held liable for [cause of action] arising from [incident]?"

**Rights Issue**:
"Whether [Party] has the right to [action] under [source of right]?"

---

## Sample Brief Answer Format

"[Direct answer]. Under [applicable law/contract provision], [standard/test]. Here, [application to facts]. Therefore, [conclusion]."

**Example**:
"Probably yes. Under Section 2-314 of the UCC, an implied warranty of merchantability arises in sales by merchants. The goods must be 'fit for the ordinary purposes for which such goods are used.' Here, the industrial pump failed within 30 days of normal use, suggesting it was not fit for its ordinary purpose. Therefore, the buyer likely has a valid warranty claim."
