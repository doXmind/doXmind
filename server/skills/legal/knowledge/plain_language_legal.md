# Plain Language in Legal Writing

Guidelines for writing clear, accessible legal documents without sacrificing precision.

---

## Why Plain Language Matters

### Benefits

- **Comprehension**: Clients and stakeholders understand their rights and obligations
- **Efficiency**: Fewer questions, less back-and-forth
- **Risk Reduction**: Misunderstandings lead to disputes
- **Compliance**: Many jurisdictions require plain language in consumer contracts
- **Accessibility**: Non-lawyers can engage with legal content

### The Shift in Legal Writing

Traditional legal writing prioritized:
- Formality over clarity
- Precedent language ("legalese")
- Complex sentence structures
- Latin phrases and archaic terms

Modern legal writing prioritizes:
- Clarity and precision
- Reader understanding
- Efficiency
- Accessibility

## Core Principles

### 1. Know Your Audience

**Questions to Ask**:
- Who will read this document?
- What is their legal sophistication?
- What do they need to understand?
- What decisions will they make based on this?

**Audience Spectrum**:
| Audience | Approach |
|----------|----------|
| Lawyers | Technical precision acceptable |
| Business executives | Clear structure, executive summaries |
| General public | Plain language essential |
| Regulators | Follow required formats, but be clear |

### 2. Use Simple Words

**Replace Archaic Terms**:

| Instead of | Use |
|------------|-----|
| Hereinafter | (use the defined term directly) |
| Whereas | (delete or use "because") |
| Aforementioned | (use "this" or repeat the noun) |
| Herein | "in this agreement" |
| Thereof | "of it" or "of [the thing]" |
| Witnesseth | (delete entirely) |
| Now, therefore | (delete entirely) |
| Said (as adjective) | "the" or "this" |
| Such (as adjective) | "the," "this," or "that" |
| Shall | "must" (for obligations), "will" (for future) |

**Replace Complex Words**:

| Instead of | Use |
|------------|-----|
| Notwithstanding | despite, even if |
| Pursuant to | under, in accordance with |
| In the event that | if |
| Prior to | before |
| Subsequent to | after |
| In lieu of | instead of |
| With respect to | about, concerning |
| For the purpose of | to |
| In order to | to |
| At this point in time | now |

### 3. Write Short Sentences

**Target**: 20-25 words average

**Techniques**:
- One idea per sentence
- Break long sentences at natural points
- Use periods, not semicolons
- Avoid multiple conditions in one sentence

**Before**:
"In the event that the Purchaser fails to make any payment when due hereunder, and such failure continues for a period of thirty (30) days after written notice thereof from the Seller, the Seller shall have the right, at its sole option, to terminate this Agreement and pursue all available remedies at law or in equity."

**After**:
"If the Purchaser fails to make a payment when due, the Seller will send written notice. If the Purchaser does not pay within 30 days of that notice, the Seller may terminate this Agreement. The Seller may also pursue any legal remedies available."

### 4. Use Active Voice

**Active voice** makes clear who does what.

| Passive | Active |
|---------|--------|
| "Payment shall be made by the Buyer" | "The Buyer must pay" |
| "Notice must be given" | "You must give notice" |
| "The Agreement may be terminated" | "Either party may terminate" |
| "Approval is required" | "The Board must approve" |

**When Passive is Acceptable**:
- When the actor is unknown or unimportant
- When you want to emphasize the action, not the actor
- When diplomacy requires it

### 5. Use Positive Statements

State what people **must do**, not what they **must not fail to do**.

| Negative | Positive |
|----------|----------|
| "shall not fail to" | "must" |
| "may not unreasonably withhold" | "must not unreasonably withhold" (or better: "must approve unless...") |
| "not invalid" | "valid" |
| "not unlike" | "similar to" or "like" |

## Structure and Organization

### Use Headings Liberally

**Good Practice**:
- Descriptive headings that summarize content
- Consistent heading hierarchy
- Numbered sections for easy reference
- Table of contents for longer documents

**Example**:
```
5. TERMINATION
   5.1 Termination for Convenience
   5.2 Termination for Cause
   5.3 Effect of Termination
   5.4 Survival
```

### Use Lists

**When to Use Lists**:
- Conditions that must all be met (use "and")
- Alternative conditions (use "or")
- Enumerated rights or obligations
- Steps in a process

**Example**:
Instead of:
"The Buyer may terminate this Agreement if the Seller materially breaches this Agreement and fails to cure such breach within thirty (30) days of notice, or if the Seller becomes insolvent, or if the Seller assigns this Agreement without consent."

Use:
"The Buyer may terminate this Agreement if:
(a) the Seller materially breaches and fails to cure within 30 days of notice;
(b) the Seller becomes insolvent; or
(c) the Seller assigns this Agreement without consent."

### Use Tables

Tables work well for:
- Pricing schedules
- Milestone dates
- Responsibility matrices
- Comparison of options or rights

## Definitions

### Best Practices

1. **Define only when necessary**
   - Don't define common words
   - Don't define terms used only once

2. **Define at first use OR in a definitions section**
   - Be consistent throughout the document
   - For complex documents, use a definitions section

3. **Use clear, intuitive defined terms**
   - "Agreement" not "this instrument"
   - "Confidential Information" not "CI"

4. **Avoid circular definitions**
   - Bad: "'Affiliate' means any Affiliated Entity"
   - Good: "'Affiliate' means any entity that controls, is controlled by, or is under common control with a party"

### Definitions Section Format

```
1. DEFINITIONS

In this Agreement:

"Affiliate" means any entity that controls, is controlled by, or is
under common control with a party. "Control" means ownership of more
than 50% of voting securities.

"Confidential Information" means any non-public information disclosed
by one party to the other, whether orally, in writing, or in any other
form.

"Effective Date" means the date first written above.
```

## Numbers and Dates

### Numbers

- Spell out one through ten
- Use numerals for 11 and above
- Use numerals for all numbers in a series
- For significant amounts, consider: "50,000 dollars ($50,000)"

### Dates

**Clear Formats**:
- "January 15, 2024" (preferred)
- "15 January 2024" (acceptable)
- Avoid: "1/15/24" (ambiguous internationally)

**Deadline Clarity**:
- "by January 15" = on or before January 15
- "within 30 days" = 30 days from triggering event
- Specify: "within 30 calendar days" vs. "within 30 business days"

## Common Problem Areas

### "And/Or"

Avoid "and/or" — it's ambiguous.

**Instead**:
- Use "or" (which typically includes "and" in legal context)
- Use "A, B, or both"
- Use "A and B, or either of them"

### "Respectively"

Often causes confusion. Rewrite to be explicit.

**Confusing**:
"The Buyer and Seller will pay $100 and $200, respectively."

**Clear**:
"The Buyer will pay $100. The Seller will pay $200."

### Time Calculations

Be explicit:
- "within 10 business days after receiving notice"
- "no later than 5:00 PM Eastern Time on the Effective Date"
- "at least 30 days before the renewal date"

## Testing Your Writing

### Readability Metrics

- **Flesch Reading Ease**: Aim for 40-60 for legal documents
- **Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level**: Aim for 12th grade or below for consumer documents

### Practical Tests

1. **Read aloud**: Does it sound natural?
2. **User test**: Can a non-lawyer understand it?
3. **One-sentence test**: Can you summarize each paragraph?
4. **So-what test**: Is every sentence necessary?

## Plain Language Checklist

### Words
- [ ] Replaced archaic legal terms
- [ ] Used simple words where possible
- [ ] Avoided jargon (or defined it)
- [ ] Used "must" instead of "shall"

### Sentences
- [ ] Average sentence length under 25 words
- [ ] One idea per sentence
- [ ] Active voice where possible
- [ ] Positive statements (what to do, not what not to do)

### Structure
- [ ] Clear headings and subheadings
- [ ] Lists for multiple items
- [ ] Tables where helpful
- [ ] Logical organization

### Overall
- [ ] Defined terms are necessary and clear
- [ ] No unnecessary legalese
- [ ] Appropriate for the audience
- [ ] Can be understood on first reading

### Key Principle

> "The language of the law must not be foreign to the ears of those who are to obey it."
> — Learned Hand

Plain language is not "dumbing down" legal writing. It's communicating legal concepts clearly and precisely so they can be understood and followed.
