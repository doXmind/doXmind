# Contract Terminology Dictionary

Essential terms and concepts for contract drafting and review.

---

## Core Contract Concepts

### Agreement Formation

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Offer** | A proposal to enter into a contract on specific terms |
| **Acceptance** | Agreement to the terms of an offer |
| **Consideration** | Something of value exchanged by each party |
| **Meeting of the Minds** | Mutual understanding and agreement on essential terms |
| **Capacity** | Legal ability to enter into a contract |
| **Legality** | The contract's purpose must be legal |

### Contract Types

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Bilateral Contract** | Both parties make promises to each other |
| **Unilateral Contract** | One party makes a promise in exchange for an act |
| **Express Contract** | Terms are explicitly stated (written or oral) |
| **Implied Contract** | Terms are inferred from conduct or circumstances |
| **Executed Contract** | All obligations have been performed |
| **Executory Contract** | Some obligations remain to be performed |

## Standard Contract Clauses

### Boilerplate Provisions

| Clause | Purpose |
|--------|---------|
| **Entire Agreement** (Integration) | States this document is the complete agreement, superseding prior discussions |
| **Amendment** | Specifies how the contract can be modified |
| **Severability** | If one provision is invalid, the rest survives |
| **Waiver** | Failure to enforce a right once doesn't waive future enforcement |
| **Assignment** | Whether/how parties can transfer their rights and obligations |
| **Governing Law** | Which jurisdiction's laws apply |
| **Dispute Resolution** | How disputes will be resolved (litigation, arbitration, mediation) |
| **Notice** | How formal communications must be delivered |
| **Force Majeure** | Excuses performance due to extraordinary events |
| **Survival** | Which provisions continue after termination |

### Rights and Obligations

| Clause | Purpose |
|--------|---------|
| **Representations** | Statements of fact at signing |
| **Warranties** | Promises that certain facts are/will be true |
| **Covenants** | Promises to do or not do something |
| **Conditions** | Events that must occur before obligations arise |
| **Indemnification** | One party agrees to cover another's losses |
| **Limitation of Liability** | Caps on damages one party can recover |

## Key Terms Defined

### A

**Acceleration Clause**: Allows the entire debt to become due immediately upon certain events (e.g., missed payment).

**Addendum**: A document added to an existing contract that modifies or supplements its terms.

**Affirmative Covenant**: A promise to do something (vs. negative covenant, a promise not to do something).

**Arbitration**: Private dispute resolution where an arbitrator (not a court) decides the outcome.

**Assignment**: Transfer of rights under a contract to another party.

**At-Will**: Either party can terminate at any time, for any reason (subject to law).

### B

**Breach**: Failure to perform a contractual obligation.

**Best Efforts**: Obligation to try hard, but not guarantee success (weaker than a warranty).

**Bona Fide**: In good faith; genuine.

**Boilerplate**: Standard provisions included in most contracts.

### C

**Cap**: A maximum limit (often on liability or fees).

**Carve-Out**: An exception to a general rule or provision.

**Change of Control**: When ownership or control of a company changes (often triggers rights).

**Closing**: The point when a transaction is completed and documents are exchanged.

**Commercially Reasonable**: Standard of effort judged by normal business practices.

**Condition Precedent**: An event that must occur before a party's obligation arises.

**Condition Subsequent**: An event that terminates an existing obligation.

**Confidentiality**: Obligation to keep certain information secret.

**Consequential Damages**: Indirect damages resulting from a breach (often excluded).

**Counterpart**: Signing the same document in separate copies, each considered an original.

**Covenant**: A binding promise to do or not do something.

**Cure Period**: Time allowed to fix a breach before consequences apply.

### D

**Default**: Failure to perform an obligation (breach).

**Delegation**: Transfer of duties (vs. assignment of rights) under a contract.

**Deliverables**: Specific items or work products to be provided.

**Disclaimer**: Language limiting or excluding warranties or liability.

**Due Diligence**: Investigation and verification before entering a transaction.

### E

**Effective Date**: When the contract becomes binding (may differ from signing date).

**Encumbrance**: A claim or lien against property.

**Equitable Relief**: Non-monetary remedies (injunction, specific performance).

**Escrow**: Funds or documents held by a third party until conditions are met.

**Exclusivity**: One party's right to be the only provider/recipient.

**Executed**: Signed; or fully performed.

**Exhibits**: Attachments that are part of the contract.

### F

**Fee Schedule**: List of prices or rates.

**Fiduciary**: Person with duty to act in another's best interest.

**Floor**: A minimum limit.

**Force Majeure**: "Superior force" — extraordinary events excusing performance (war, natural disasters).

**Forfeit**: Lose a right, often as penalty.

### G

**Good Faith**: Honesty and fair dealing.

**Governing Law**: The jurisdiction whose laws will interpret the contract.

**Grace Period**: Extra time to perform without penalty.

**Guaranty/Guarantee**: Promise to pay or perform if another party doesn't.

### H

**Holdback**: Portion of payment withheld until conditions are met.

**Holdover**: Continuing past the term (e.g., a tenant staying after lease ends).

### I

**Indemnification**: Agreement to compensate for losses or damages.

**Injunction**: Court order requiring or prohibiting an action.

**Intellectual Property (IP)**: Creations of the mind (patents, copyrights, trademarks, trade secrets).

**Interest**: A right, share, or stake in something.

### J

**Joint and Several**: Each party is fully responsible for the whole obligation.

**Jurisdiction**: Geographic area or court system with authority over a dispute.

### K

**Key Person**: Individual whose involvement is essential (may trigger rights if they leave).

### L

**Lien**: A legal claim against property as security for a debt.

**Limitation of Liability**: Clause capping damages one party can recover.

**Liquidated Damages**: Pre-agreed amount payable upon breach.

### M

**Material**: Significant; important enough to affect decisions.

**Material Adverse Change (MAC)**: A significant negative change (often a condition or termination trigger).

**Mediation**: Facilitated negotiation with a neutral third party (non-binding).

**Merger Clause**: Same as Entire Agreement / Integration clause.

**Milestone**: A specific achievement or date triggering obligations.

**Most Favored Nation (MFN)**: Promise to give terms at least as good as given to others.

### N

**Net**: Amount after deductions.

**Non-Compete**: Agreement not to compete with the other party.

**Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA)**: Contract protecting confidential information.

**Non-Solicitation**: Agreement not to recruit or do business with certain parties.

**Notice**: Formal communication required by the contract.

### O

**Obligation**: A duty to do or not do something.

**Offset**: Reducing a payment by amounts owed in the other direction.

**Option**: Right, but not obligation, to take an action.

### P

**Party**: A person or entity that signs the contract.

**Penalty**: Punishment for breach (often unenforceable if excessive).

**Performance**: Fulfilling contractual obligations.

**Preamble**: Introduction identifying parties and context (the "whereas" section).

**Precedent**: A condition that must be satisfied before obligations arise.

**Pro Rata**: Proportionally.

### R

**Recitals**: Background statements (often starting with "Whereas").

**Remedies**: Relief available for breach (damages, specific performance, etc.).

**Renewal**: Extension of the term.

**Representation**: Statement of fact (as of a specific date).

**Rescission**: Canceling a contract, returning parties to pre-contract position.

**Restrictive Covenant**: Promise limiting what a party can do.

**Right of First Refusal (ROFR)**: Right to match any offer before it's accepted.

**Royalty**: Payment for use of intellectual property.

### S

**Schedule**: Attachment with detailed information (often lists, prices, specifications).

**Scope**: The boundaries of what's included.

**Security Interest**: Right to take property if obligations aren't met.

**Set-Off**: See Offset.

**Several**: Each party is responsible only for their own share (vs. joint and several).

**Specific Performance**: Court order requiring a party to perform their obligations.

**Statute of Limitations**: Time limit for bringing legal claims.

**Subcontract**: Hiring a third party to perform obligations.

**Subordination**: Agreeing that another party's rights take priority.

**Survival**: Provisions that continue after termination.

### T

**Term**: The duration of the contract.

**Termination**: Ending the contract.

**Termination for Cause**: Ending due to the other party's breach.

**Termination for Convenience**: Ending without need to show breach.

**Time is of the Essence**: Deadlines are strictly enforced.

**Title**: Ownership rights.

**Tolling**: Pausing the running of a time period.

### U

**Unconscionable**: So unfair that a court won't enforce it.

### V

**Venue**: The specific location where disputes will be heard.

**Void**: Not legally enforceable from the start.

**Voidable**: Can be cancelled by one party (e.g., due to fraud or incapacity).

### W

**Waiver**: Giving up a right.

**Warranty**: Promise that certain facts are or will be true.

**Work Product**: Materials created in performing the contract.

|------|---------|-------|
| **Ab initio** | From the beginning | "The contract is void ab initio" |
| **Bona fide** | In good faith | "A bona fide purchaser" |

## Standard of Care Terms

| Term | Meaning | Strength |
|------|---------|----------|
| **Best Efforts** | Maximum possible effort | Highest |
| **Reasonable Best Efforts** | High effort, but with reason | High |
| **Commercially Reasonable Efforts** | Effort typical in the industry | Medium-High |
| **Reasonable Efforts** | What a reasonable person would do | Medium |
| **Good Faith Efforts** | Honest attempt | Medium |
| **Efforts** (unqualified) | Some attempt | Lower |

## Quick Reference: Words to Avoid

| Avoid | Use Instead |
|-------|-------------|
| Shall | Must (obligation), Will (future) |
| Herein | In this agreement |
| Hereinafter | [Just use the defined term] |
| Whereas | [Delete or use "Because"] |
| Said [noun] | The [noun], This [noun] |
| Such [noun] | The [noun], That [noun] |
| Therein | In it, In that [document/section] |
| Thereof | Of it, Of that [thing] |
| Hereby | [Usually can be deleted] |
| Witnesseth | [Delete entirely] |

## Contract Structure Reference

### Typical Order

1. **Title**: Name of the agreement
2. **Preamble**: Parties, date, defined short names
3. **Recitals**: Background and context ("Whereas...")
4. **Definitions**: Defined terms
5. **Operative Provisions**: The actual deal terms
6. **Representations and Warranties**: Statements of fact
7. **Covenants**: Ongoing obligations
8. **Conditions**: What must happen before/after
9. **Termination**: When and how it ends
10. **Indemnification**: Who covers what losses
11. **General Provisions**: Boilerplate
12. **Signature Block**: Names, titles, dates
13. **Exhibits/Schedules**: Attachments
