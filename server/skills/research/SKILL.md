## Research Process Workflow

### 1. Defining the Research Question

A good research question is:
- **Clear**: Specific and unambiguous
- **Focused**: Narrow enough to be answered thoroughly
- **Complex**: Requires analysis, not just description
- **Arguable**: Has multiple possible answers
- **Relevant**: Contributes to the field

#### Research Question Frameworks

**PICO Framework** (for clinical/quantitative research):
- **P**opulation: Who is being studied?
- **I**ntervention: What is being done?
- **C**omparison: What is it compared to?
- **O**utcome: What is being measured?

**PICOT Framework** (adds Time):
- **P**opulation: Who is being studied?
- **I**ntervention: What is being done?
- **C**omparison: What is it compared to?
- **O**utcome: What is being measured?
- **T**ime: Over what time period?

**PICOS Framework** (adds Study type):
- **P**opulation: Who is being studied?
- **I**ntervention: What is being done?
- **C**omparison: What is it compared to?
- **O**utcome: What is being measured?
- **S**tudy type: What study designs to include?

**SPIDER Framework** (for qualitative/mixed methods):
- **S**ample: The group being studied
- **P**henomenon of **I**nterest: What behavior or experience is being explored?
- **D**esign: The research design (e.g., interview, case study)
- **E**valuation: The outcomes being measured
- **R**esearch type: Qualitative, quantitative, or mixed methods

**SPICE Framework** (for evaluating outcomes):
- **S**etting: Where is the study conducted?
- **P**erspective: Whose viewpoint is being considered?
- **I**ntervention: What is being done?
- **C**omparison: What is it compared to?
- **E**valuation: How are outcomes measured?

**When to use each framework:**
| Framework | Best For |
|-----------|----------|
| PICO | Clinical trials, quantitative intervention studies |
| PICOT | Time-sensitive outcomes, longitudinal studies |
| PICOS | Systematic reviews needing study type filters |
| SPIDER | Qualitative research, mixed methods studies |
| SPICE | Service evaluations, policy research |

### 2. Conducting a Literature Review

**Steps:**
1. Define search terms and databases
2. Set inclusion/exclusion criteria
3. Screen titles and abstracts
4. Full-text review of selected papers
5. Extract and synthesize findings
6. Identify themes and gaps

**Organization Options:**
- **Thematic**: Group by topic or theme
- **Chronological**: Trace development over time
- **Methodological**: Compare different approaches

### 3. Selecting Research Methodology

**Qualitative Methods:**
- Interviews (structured, semi-structured, unstructured)
- Focus groups
- Ethnography
- Case studies
- Grounded theory
- Phenomenology

**Quantitative Methods:**
- Surveys/questionnaires
- Experiments (RCTs, quasi-experimental)
- Correlational studies
- Longitudinal studies
- Cross-sectional studies

**Mixed Methods:**
- Sequential explanatory
- Sequential exploratory
- Convergent parallel
- Embedded design

### 4. Data Collection and Sampling

**Sampling Strategies:**
- **Probability**: Random, stratified, cluster, systematic
- **Non-probability**: Convenience, purposive, snowball, quota

**Sample Size Considerations:**
- Power analysis for quantitative studies
- Saturation point for qualitative studies
- Effect size and significance level

### 5. Data Analysis

**Quantitative Analysis:**
- Descriptive statistics (mean, median, mode, SD)
- Inferential statistics (t-tests, ANOVA, chi-square, regression)
- Effect sizes and confidence intervals

**Qualitative Analysis:**
- Thematic analysis
- Content analysis
- Narrative analysis
- Grounded theory coding (open, axial, selective)

### 6. Writing Up Research (IMRAD)

**Introduction**
- Background and context
- Literature review
- Research gap
- Research questions/hypotheses
- Objectives

**Methods**
- Research design
- Participants/sample
- Data collection procedures
- Data analysis approach
- Ethical considerations

**Results**
- Present findings objectively
- Use tables and figures
- Report statistical tests
- Don't interpret here

**Discussion**
- Interpret findings
- Compare with existing literature
- Discuss implications
- Acknowledge limitations
- Suggest future research

## Identifying Research Gaps

**Types of Gaps:**
- **Evidence Gap**: Limited empirical research
- **Knowledge Gap**: Theoretical understanding missing
- **Practical Gap**: Theory-practice disconnect
- **Methodological Gap**: Better methods needed
- **Population Gap**: Understudied groups

## Ethical Considerations

- Informed consent
- Confidentiality and anonymity
- Data protection
- Avoiding harm
- Research integrity
- Institutional review board (IRB) approval

## Guidelines

When assisting with research:
- ALWAYS clarify the research question and objectives first
- Consider the appropriate methodology for the research goals
- Emphasize validity, reliability, and ethical considerations
- Help identify potential biases and limitations
- Guide proper interpretation of findings
- Support clear and accurate presentation of results

## Common Research Pitfalls

1. **Confirmation bias** - Seeking evidence that supports your hypothesis
2. **Selection bias** - Non-representative sample
3. **Publication bias** - Overreporting positive results
4. **p-hacking** - Manipulating analysis to achieve significance
5. **Overgeneralization** - Extending findings beyond the sample
6. **Correlation ≠ Causation** - Misinterpreting relationships
