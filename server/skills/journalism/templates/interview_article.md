# Interview Article Template

Structure for stories based primarily on interviews, including Q&A format and narrative interview pieces.

---

## Option 1: Q&A Format

### Headline

**[Subject Name] on [Topic]: "[Key Quote or Theme]"**

**Examples**:
- "Jane Doe on Climate Tech: 'We Have 10 Years to Get This Right'"
- "The Coach Who Rebuilt the Program: A Conversation with John Smith"
- "Inside the Lab: Dr. Chen Discusses the Future of Gene Therapy"

### Introduction

**[PUBLICATION NAME] Interview**

*[Subject Name] is [brief identification—title, why they matter]. [Context for why we're talking to them now—new book, project, event, milestone]. [What readers will learn from this conversation].*

**Length**: 2-4 paragraphs

**Example**:
"Maria Santos has spent 30 years fighting for environmental justice in communities most people have forgotten. As the founder of GreenFuture Alliance, she's won landmark cases against polluters and trained a generation of activists.

Her new book, 'Breathing Room,' chronicles her journey from community organizer to national figure. We spoke with Santos about what's changed—and what hasn't—in the fight for clean air and water."

### Interview Context (Optional)

*This interview has been edited and condensed for clarity.*

*or*

*This interview was conducted on [date] at [location/via phone/video].*

---

### The Q&A

**[Interviewer or Publication Name]: [Question]**

[Full answer from subject, edited for clarity but preserving their voice and meaning]

**[Interviewer]: [Follow-up or new question]**

[Answer]

---

#### Q&A Best Practices

**Opening Question**
Start with something engaging that sets up the conversation:
- Why now? (What's the occasion?)
- Origin story (How did you get here?)
- Defining moment (What changed everything?)

**Middle Questions**
- Dig into specifics
- Ask for examples
- Challenge assumptions (respectfully)
- Follow up on interesting points
- Cover the essential topics

**Closing Question**
End with something forward-looking or reflective:
- What's next?
- What do you hope for?
- What would you tell your younger self?
- What do you want readers to remember?

---

### Editing Guidelines

**What to Edit**
- Remove filler words (um, uh, like, you know)
- Fix obvious grammatical errors
- Remove false starts and repetition
- Tighten for clarity

**What to Preserve**
- The subject's voice and personality
- Specific details and examples
- Emotional moments
- Unique phrasing that reveals character

**What to Avoid**
- Changing meaning
- Making them sound "better" or "worse"
- Removing important context
- Adding words they didn't say

### Opening Scene

[Set the scene. Where did the interview take place? What was the subject doing? Include sensory details that reveal character.]

**Example**:
"Maria Santos is running late. At 67, she still keeps a schedule that would exhaust someone half her age—this morning alone, she's addressed a city council meeting, taken three phone calls with lawyers, and somehow found time to make tamales for her grandson's birthday.

'I'll sleep when I'm dead,' she says, settling into a chair in her small office. The walls are covered with photographs: Santos with three presidents, with activists, with the families of communities she's helped. 'Actually, I'll probably keep working then too.'"

### The Story

[Weave together narrative, quotes, and context. Unlike Q&A, you're telling a story about the person, using the interview as your primary source.]

#### Technique: Integrate Quotes into Narrative

**Clunky**: I asked her about her early career. She said, "I never planned to become an activist."

**Better**: Santos never planned to become an activist. "I was a nurse," she says. "I just kept seeing the same kids with the same asthma, the same cancer. At some point, you have to ask why."

---

### Section Structure

**Section 1: The Hook**
- Opening scene
- Who is this person?
- Why should we care?

**Section 2: The Background**
- How did they get here?
- Key moments in their story
- Context and history

**Section 3: The Present**
- What are they doing now?
- What challenges do they face?
- What drives them?

**Section 4: The Meaning**
- What does their story tell us?
- What's at stake?
- What's next?

**Closing Scene**
- Return to the present moment
- A revealing detail or quote
- Leave readers with something to think about

## Interview Preparation

### Before the Interview

**Research**
- [ ] Read previous interviews and profiles
- [ ] Review their work, achievements, controversies
- [ ] Understand the context for this interview
- [ ] Identify gaps in public knowledge

**Logistics**
- [ ] Confirm date, time, location/platform
- [ ] Clarify length of interview
- [ ] Test recording equipment
- [ ] Prepare backup recording method

**Questions**
- [ ] Prepare 10-15 main questions
- [ ] Prepare follow-up questions
- [ ] Start with easier questions, build to harder ones
- [ ] End with strong closing question

### During the Interview

**Best Practices**
- Listen actively—follow up on interesting points
- Let silences happen—people often fill them with gold
- Ask for specifics: "Can you give me an example?"
- Ask for clarification: "What do you mean by that?"
- Be willing to deviate from your plan

**Questions to Always Ask**
- "Can you tell me about a specific moment when...?"
- "What do you mean by [term/phrase they used]?"
- "How did that make you feel?"
- "What did you learn from that?"
- "Is there anything I haven't asked that I should have?"

### After the Interview

- [ ] Review recording/notes immediately
- [ ] Mark strongest quotes
- [ ] Identify gaps to follow up on
- [ ] Send thank-you if appropriate
- [ ] Verify quotes before publication

## Quote Selection Criteria

### Use Quotes That:

- Reveal character or personality
- Provide insight only this person could give
- Are memorable or well-phrased
- Convey emotion
- Advance the story

### Paraphrase When:

- The information is straightforward
- Your summary is clearer
- The quote is too long
- Multiple quotes say similar things

### Example

**Original quote**: "I think what people don't understand is that this work is really hard and takes a really long time and you don't always see results and that can be discouraging but you have to keep going anyway."

**Better as paraphrase with key quote**: The work is hard, Santos acknowledges, and results can take years. "You have to keep going anyway," she says.
