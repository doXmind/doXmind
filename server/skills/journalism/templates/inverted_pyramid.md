# Inverted Pyramid News Article Template

Standard structure for hard news stories, with most important information first.

---

## Headline

**[Active, Present-Tense Headline That Summarizes the News]**

Guidelines:
- Use present tense for current events
- Be specific and informative
- Keep to 5-10 words
- Include key subject and action
- Omit articles (a, an, the) if needed

**Examples**:
- "City Council Approves $50 Million Transit Plan"
- "Tech Giant Announces 500 Layoffs Amid Slowdown"
- "Local Team Wins State Championship in Overtime"

## The Lead (First Paragraph)

[The lead answers the most essential questions: WHO did WHAT, WHEN, WHERE, and (if immediately clear) WHY and HOW. It should be one sentence, typically 25-35 words, that captures the essence of the story.]

**Structure**: [Subject] [action verb] [object/complement] [time element] [location if relevant].

**Example**:
"The City Council voted unanimously Tuesday night to approve a $50 million expansion of the downtown transit system, ending months of debate over the project's funding."

**Lead Types**:
- **Summary lead**: Standard who-what-when-where
- **Blind lead**: Delays identifying the subject for effect
- **Question lead**: Poses a question (use sparingly)
- **Quote lead**: Opens with powerful quote (rare)

---

## Second Paragraph: The "Nut Graf"

[This paragraph provides essential context, explains why the story matters, or adds the most important detail not in the lead. It tells readers why they should care.]

**Example**:
"The approval marks the largest infrastructure investment in the city's history and is expected to reduce commute times by up to 20 percent for thousands of daily riders in the eastern suburbs."

---

## Supporting Details (Paragraphs 3-5)

### Key Details

[Expand on the lead with the most important supporting information]

- What specifically happened?
- Who are the key players?
- What are the immediate implications?

### First Quote

[Include a quote from a primary source that adds value—insight, emotion, or authority—not just information you could paraphrase better]

"[Quote that provides perspective or adds human element]," said [Full Name], [title/credential].

**Quote Guidelines**:
- Use quotes that add voice, not basic facts
- Partial quotes can be used for key phrases
- Attribution typically follows the quote
- Use "said" as default verb

---

## Context and Background (Paragraphs 6-8)

### Background

[Provide relevant history and context that helps readers understand the significance]

- How did we get here?
- What's the broader context?
- What previous events led to this?

### Additional Perspectives

[Include other viewpoints, especially on controversial topics]

"[Quote from another source with different perspective]," said [Full Name], [title].

**Balance Guidelines**:
- Include opposing views on controversial issues
- Ensure fair representation
- Avoid false equivalence

---

### Secondary Information

[Include important but less essential details that readers who continue will want to know]

- Technical details
- Statistics and data
- Related developments

### More Quotes

[Additional quotes that provide depth]

### Future Implications

[What happens next? What should readers watch for?]

## The Kicker (Final Paragraph)

[End with forward-looking information, a telling detail, or a quote that provides closure without being editorial]

**Options**:
- What comes next
- A revealing detail
- A quote that encapsulates the situation
- Information about where to get more details

## Inverted Pyramid Visual Guide

```
      /\
     /  \      ← LEAD: Who, What, When, Where (most important)
    /    \
   /------\    ← NUT GRAF: Why it matters
  /        \
 /----------\  ← KEY DETAILS: Supporting information
/            \
--------------  ← CONTEXT: Background, other perspectives
|            |
|            |  ← ADDITIONAL DETAILS: Secondary information
|            |
|____________|  ← KICKER: What's next, closing detail
```
