# AP Style Quick Reference

Essential Associated Press style guidelines for news writing.

---

## Numbers

### General Rule

- Spell out **one through nine**
- Use numerals for **10 and above**

### Exceptions (Always Use Numerals)

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| Ages | 5-year-old girl, The boy, 8, said... |
| Percent | 3 percent (not three percent) |
| Money | $5, $5 million |
| Dimensions | 5 feet tall, 9-by-12 room |
| Speeds | 6 mph |
| Temperatures | 8 degrees |
| Times | 9 a.m. |
| Dates | Jan. 5 |
| Addresses | 5 Main St. |
| Scores | The team won 5-3 |
| Votes | The measure passed 5-4 |

### Large Numbers

- Use figures with million, billion: **$5 million, 3 billion**
- Spell out million/billion: **5 million** (not 5,000,000)
- For ranges: **$5 million to $10 million** (repeat million)

### Starting Sentences

- Spell out numbers at the start of sentences
- Or rewrite to avoid: "Twenty people attended" → "The event drew 20 people"

## Dates and Times

### Dates

- Month Day, Year: **January 15, 2024** (comma after day and year)
- Month Day: **January 15** (no comma)
- Abbreviate months with dates: **Jan. 15, Feb. 3, Aug. 20**
- Don't abbreviate: March, April, May, June, July (too short)
- Years alone: **2024** (no comma before or after unless grammar requires)

### Days

- Don't abbreviate days of the week in running text
- OK to abbreviate in tabular material: Mon., Tue., Wed., etc.

### Time

- Use **a.m.** and **p.m.** (lowercase, periods)
- Use figures: **9 a.m., 2:30 p.m.**
- Noon and midnight: **12 noon** is redundant; just use **noon** or **midnight**
- Time ranges: **9 a.m. to 5 p.m.** or **9-11 a.m.**

### Decades and Centuries

- The 1990s (no apostrophe)
- The '90s (apostrophe replaces missing figures)
- 21st century (no hyphen unless adjective: 21st-century technology)

## Titles

### Before Names

Capitalize and abbreviate:
- **Dr.** Jane Smith (for medical doctors only)
- **Gov.** Jane Smith
- **Sen.** Jane Smith
- **Rep.** Jane Smith
- **Lt.** Jane Smith

Don't abbreviate:
- **President** Jane Smith
- **Professor** Jane Smith
- **Superintendent** Jane Smith

### After Names or Alone

Lowercase:
- Jane Smith, **the governor**, said...
- The **president** spoke...
- She is a **doctor**

### Formal Titles

- Capitalize formal titles before names: **Mayor Jane Smith**
- Lowercase job descriptions: **astronaut John Glenn**

---

## Names

### First Reference

Use full name with title if applicable:
- **President Joe Biden**
- **Dr. Jane Smith**
- **Jane Smith, CEO of Acme Corp.**

### Second Reference

Use last name only:
- **Biden** said...
- **Smith** added...

### Courtesy Titles

- Generally, don't use Mr., Mrs., Ms.
- Exceptions: Some publications use courtesy titles on second reference
- Use only when needed to distinguish: Mr. and Mrs. Smith

## Punctuation

### Comma

**Serial comma (Oxford comma)**:
- AP does NOT use: **red, white and blue** (not red, white, and blue)
- Exception: Use when needed for clarity: **I love my parents, Batman and Wonder Woman** should be **I love my parents, Batman, and Wonder Woman**

**With conjunctions**:
- Use comma before conjunction in compound sentences
- **The bill passed, and the governor signed it.**

### Quotation Marks

- Periods and commas **inside** quotes
- Colons and semicolons **outside** quotes
- Question marks and exclamation points: inside if part of quote, outside if not

### Apostrophe

- Possessive singular: **the girl's hat**
- Possessive plural: **the girls' hats**
- Plural of single letters: **Mind your p's and q's**
- Decades: **the 1990s** (no apostrophe), **the '90s** (apostrophe for omitted figures)

### Hyphen

**Compound modifiers before nouns**:
- A **well-known** author (but: The author is **well known**)
- A **first-quarter** touchdown
- **Full-time** employees

**Don't hyphenate**:
- Adverbs ending in -ly: **a newly built house**
- Proper nouns: **New York style**

### Em Dash

- Use to set off a phrase: **The mayor—who took office last year—said...**
- Use for abrupt change: **She was going to—well, never mind.**
- No spaces around em dash

## Capitalization

### Titles of Works

- Books, movies, albums, TV shows: **Capitalize principal words**, use quotation marks
  - **"The Great Gatsby"**
  - **"Star Wars"**
- Newspapers, magazines: Capitalize, no quotes
  - **The New York Times**
  - **Time magazine**

### Government

- **Congress** (U.S. Congress)
- **the House, the Senate**
- **federal government** (lowercase)
- **the White House**
- **the administration** (lowercase)

### Geography

- **the West, the Midwest, the South, the East** (regions)
- **western states, southern California** (directions/adjectives)
- **Earth** (when used as proper noun, no "the")
- **the sun, the moon** (lowercase)

### Other

- **internet** (lowercase)
- **website** (lowercase, one word)
- **email** (lowercase, no hyphen)
- **OK** (not okay)

## Attribution

### Said Is Standard

- Use **said** as the default attribution verb
- Attribution typically follows the quote

**Correct**: "The bill will pass," the governor said.

### Avoid Editorializing

| Avoid | Why |
|-------|-----|
| claimed | Implies doubt |
| admitted | Implies wrongdoing |
| refuted | Implies they successfully disproved |
| revealed | Implies something was hidden |

### Placement

- **Short quotes**: Attribution can come first
  - Smith said the bill "will definitely pass."
- **Full quotes**: Attribution typically follows
  - "The bill will pass," Smith said.
- **Long quotes**: Break with attribution
  - "The bill will pass," Smith said. "We have the votes."
