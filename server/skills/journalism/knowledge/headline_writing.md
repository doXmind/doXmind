# Headline Writing Guide

Craft compelling headlines that inform, engage, and accurately represent your story.

---

## Headline Functions

A good headline should:

1. **Summarize** the story's main point
2. **Attract** readers to the story
3. **Inform** those who only read headlines
4. **Set tone** for the article
5. **Optimize** for search and social (in digital)

---

## Basic Headline Rules

### Grammar and Style

**Present Tense for Current Events**
- "City Council **Approves** Budget" (not approved)
- "Storm **Kills** 5" (not killed)

**Future Tense Uses Infinitive**
- "Mayor **to Resign** Friday"
- "Teams **to Meet** in Finals"

**Omit Articles When Needed**
- "Mayor Proposes Budget" (not "The Mayor")
- Space-saving technique

**Active Voice**
- "Police Arrest Suspect" (not "Suspect Arrested by Police")
- Stronger, clearer, shorter

**Avoid Forms of "To Be"**
- Instead of "Fire Is Investigated," use "Investigators Probe Fire"
- More dynamic

### Numbers and Abbreviations

**Use Numerals**
- "5 Injured in Crash" (not Five)
- Exception: Starting a headline with a number is OK

**Abbreviations**
- OK: FBI, CIA, UN, US
- Spell out when space allows
- Avoid unfamiliar abbreviations

## Types of Headlines

### Hard News Headlines

Straightforward, factual, summarizes the news.

**Formula**: [Subject] [Verb] [Object/Detail]

**Examples**:
- "Senate Passes $1 Trillion Infrastructure Bill"
- "Hurricane Makes Landfall in Florida"
- "Apple Reports Record Quarterly Profit"

### Feature Headlines

Creative, intriguing, often uses wordplay or questions.

**Examples**:
- "The Last Lighthouse Keeper"
- "Where Have All the Bees Gone?"
- "A Second Chance at 72"

### Question Headlines

Engage readers, promise answers.

**Examples**:
- "Is This the End of the Office?"
- "What Happened to Flight 370?"

**Caution**: Question should intrigue, not annoy. If answer is "no," don't ask.

### How-To Headlines

Promise practical value.

**Examples**:
- "How to Survive a Bear Attack"
- "5 Ways to Cut Your Tax Bill"

### Quote Headlines

Use compelling quote as headline.

**Examples**:
- "'We Will Rebuild,' Mayor Vows"
- "'This Changes Everything,' Scientists Say"

## Headline Writing Techniques

### Be Specific

**Vague**: "Economy Shows Improvement"
**Specific**: "Unemployment Falls to 4%, Lowest in 50 Years"

**Vague**: "Weather Causes Problems"
**Specific**: "Blizzard Strands 500 at Airport"

### Use Strong Verbs

| Weak | Strong |
|------|--------|
| Is looking into | Probes, Investigates |
| Will try to | Aims to |
| Makes an effort | Pushes, Seeks |
| Comes to an agreement | Agrees |
| Had an effect on | Affects, Changes |

**Power Verbs for Headlines**:
Aims, Backs, Bars, Bids, Cites, Eyes, Faces, Fuels, Grips, Halts, Hikes, Hits, Marks, Nears, Nets, Oks, Outs, Probes, Raps, Rips, Seeks, Sets, Slams, Sparks, Spurs, Taps, Tops, Unveils, Vows, Weighs

### Create Urgency

- "Climate Bill Faces Final Vote Today"
- "Deadline Looms for Tax Relief"
- "Last Chance to Save Historic Theater"

### Use Numbers

- "5 Dead in Factory Fire"
- "3 Tips for Better Sleep"
- "1 in 4 Americans Struggles With..."

Numbers are eye-catching and specific.

## Digital Headlines

### SEO Considerations

**Include Keywords**
- Think about what people would search for
- Front-load important words
- "iPhone 15 Review: Apple's Best Yet" (not "Apple's Best Yet: Our iPhone 15 Review")

**Be Clear, Not Clever**
- Search engines don't understand puns
- Balance creativity with clarity

### Social Media Headlines

**Character Limits**
- Twitter/X: ~70-100 characters ideal
- Facebook: ~40-80 characters
- LinkedIn: ~40-49 characters

**Engagement Tactics**
- Ask questions
- Use numbers
- Create curiosity gap (but don't be clickbait)
- Emotional resonance

### Click-Worthy vs. Clickbait

**Click-Worthy** (Good):
- Accurate representation of content
- Creates genuine interest
- Delivers on promise

**Clickbait** (Bad):
- Misleading or exaggerated
- "You Won't Believe..."
- Doesn't deliver on promise
- Manipulative emotion

## Headline Formulas

### News Headline Formula

**[Who] [What/Action] [Result/Detail]**
- "Congress Passes Bill to Limit..."
- "Fire Destroys 50 Homes in..."

### Feature Headline Formulas

**The [Noun] Who/That [Surprising Thing]**
- "The Town That Banned Cars"
- "The Scientist Who Talks to Whales"

**[Number] [Things] About [Topic]**
- "5 Things We Learned From..."
- "10 Facts About..."

**How [Person/Group] [Did Something]**
- "How One Teacher Changed a School"
- "How Silicon Valley Lost Its Way"

**The [Adjective] Story of [Subject]**
- "The Untold Story of the Moon Landing"
- "The Strange Story of a Missing..."

### Question Formula

**What/Why/How + [Intriguing Element]?**
- "Why Can't We Sleep?"
- "What Happened to the American Dream?"

## Headline Checklist

### Accuracy
- [ ] Headline accurately represents the story
- [ ] No exaggeration or misleading claims
- [ ] Facts in headline are verified
- [ ] Attribution clear if needed

### Clarity
- [ ] Main point is clear
- [ ] No confusing abbreviations
- [ ] No jargon (or jargon is explained)
- [ ] Grammar is correct

### Engagement
- [ ] Strong, active verb
- [ ] Specific details
- [ ] Compelling angle
- [ ] Appropriate for audience

### Technical
- [ ] Fits character/space limits
- [ ] SEO keywords included (digital)
- [ ] Works without subhead
- [ ] Present tense for current news

## Common Mistakes

### Burying the Lead

**Bad**: "Mayor Speaks at Event, Announces Resignation"
**Good**: "Mayor Announces Resignation"

### Being Too Vague

**Bad**: "Changes Coming to Area"
**Good**: "Highway Expansion to Close Exit for 6 Months"

### Misleading

**Bad**: "Cure for Cancer Found" (for early research)
**Good**: "Scientists Find Promising Cancer Treatment Target"

### Using Clichés

**Avoid**:
- "Breaks Silence"
- "Slams" (overused)
- "At the End of the Day"
- "Perfect Storm"
- "Wake-Up Call"

### Passive Voice

**Bad**: "Man Killed by Lightning"
**Good**: "Lightning Kills Man"

## Subheadlines (Decks)

### Purpose

- Add context
- Expand on headline
- Answer follow-up questions
- Improve SEO

### Format

1-2 sentences that complement, don't repeat, the headline.

**Headline**: "Tech Giant Announces 500 Layoffs"
**Deck**: "The cuts represent 10% of the workforce and come amid slowing demand for the company's products."

## Practice Exercise

### Rewrite These Headlines

**Original**: "A Study Has Found That People Who Exercise Live Longer"
**Better**: "Exercise Linked to Longer Life, Study Finds"

**Original**: "There Was a Fire at a Factory Last Night"
**Better**: "Factory Fire Injures 3 Workers"

**Original**: "The President Said He Might Veto the Bill"
**Better**: "President Threatens to Veto Tax Bill"

## Quick Reference

### News Headlines
- Present tense
- Active voice
- Specific details
- Strong verbs
- 5-10 words typical

### Feature Headlines
- Can be creative
- Intrigue or question OK
- Less formulaic
- Still must be accurate

### Digital Headlines
- Front-load keywords
- Be clear for search
- Consider social sharing
- Avoid pure clickbait
