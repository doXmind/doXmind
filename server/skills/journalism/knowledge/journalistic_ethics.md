# Journalistic Ethics

Core principles and guidelines for ethical journalism.

---

## Foundational Principles

### Society of Professional Journalists Code of Ethics

The SPJ Code identifies four main principles:

1. **Seek Truth and Report It**
2. **Minimize Harm**
3. **Act Independently**
4. **Be Accountable and Transparent**

---

## Seek Truth and Report It

### Accuracy

- **Verify information** before publishing
- **Use multiple sources** for significant claims
- **Identify sources** clearly when possible
- **Acknowledge errors** and correct them promptly
- **Never plagiarize** or fabricate
- **Don't alter photos** in misleading ways

### Completeness

- Provide **context** for facts
- Report **multiple perspectives** on complex issues
- Don't omit information that changes meaning
- Pursue stories with **significance**, not just interest

### Fairness

- Give subjects **opportunity to respond** to allegations
- Don't misrepresent through **selective editing**
- Present information **in context**
- Avoid **false equivalence** (treating unequal claims equally)

### Transparency About Methods

- Be upfront about how information was gathered
- Disclose use of unnamed sources (and why)
- Explain limitations of your reporting

---

## Minimize Harm

### Sensitivity

- Balance the public's need to know against potential harm
- Show **compassion** for those affected by coverage
- Be especially careful with:
  - Victims of trauma
  - Children
  - Inexperienced sources
  - Grieving people

### Privacy

- Recognize that private people have greater privacy rights than public figures
- Consider whether information is truly in public interest
- **Think before naming**:
  - Victims of sexual assault (generally don't identify)
  - Juveniles (generally protected)
  - Suicide details (minimize method description)
  - Private citizens in embarrassing situations

### Avoiding Harm

- Consider **secondary trauma** from graphic content
- Weigh whether images or details are necessary
- Consider impact on families of subjects
- Don't exploit grief for dramatic effect

## Act Independently

### Avoid Conflicts of Interest

**Financial Conflicts**
- Don't report on companies you own stock in
- Disclose financial relationships
- Don't accept gifts that could influence coverage

**Personal Conflicts**
- Don't cover family members or close friends
- Disclose personal relationships that may affect coverage
- Recuse yourself when objectivity is compromised

**Political Conflicts**
- Avoid political activism while reporting on politics
- Don't make political donations if covering politics
- Keep personal views out of straight news reporting

### Resist Pressure

- Don't let sources dictate coverage
- Resist pressure from advertisers
- Don't trade favorable coverage for access
- Maintain editorial independence from business interests

### Access and Favoritism

- Don't let access become dependency
- Be willing to lose access to maintain integrity
- Apply same standards to all subjects

---

## Be Accountable and Transparent

### Corrections

- Correct errors **promptly and prominently**
- Acknowledge the nature of the error
- Don't delete online errors—note corrections

**Correction Format**:
"An earlier version of this article misstated [what was wrong]. [Correct information]. The article has been updated."

### Respond to Questions

- Explain your reporting decisions when asked
- Be open about your methods
- Engage with legitimate criticism
- Distinguish between criticism and harassment

### Acknowledge Limitations

- Be honest about what you don't know
- Note when information is unverified
- Disclose conflicts and constraints

## Sources

### Named vs. Anonymous Sources

**Prefer Named Sources**
- Named sources allow readers to evaluate credibility
- Anonymous sources should be used sparingly
- Readers trust named sources more

**When Anonymous Sources Are Acceptable**
- Information is vital to public interest
- Source faces genuine risk (job loss, safety)
- Information cannot be obtained any other way
- Information has been verified independently

**Rules for Anonymous Sources**
- Editor should know the source's identity
- Explain why the source can't be named
- Describe source as specifically as possible ("a senior White House official" not "a source")
- Verify the information through other means

### Protecting Sources

- Honor confidentiality agreements
- Understand the legal landscape (shield laws vary)
- Be prepared to protect sources even under pressure
- Don't promise confidentiality casually

### Source Relationships

- Be professional, not friends
- Don't become part of the story
- Maintain appropriate boundaries
- Be transparent about the relationship with readers

## Specific Situations

### Reporting on Crime

**Suspects**
- Use "alleged" or "accused" before conviction
- Avoid implying guilt before trial
- Include context about charges and denials

**Victims**
- Generally don't identify sexual assault victims
- Get consent before identifying victims of other crimes
- Consider whether identification serves public interest

**Ongoing Investigations**
- Be careful not to interfere with investigations
- Consider whether reporting could harm cases
- Weigh public interest against potential harm

### Reporting on Minors

- Extra protections for children
- Obtain parental consent when possible
- Consider long-term impact of coverage
- Protect identity in sensitive cases

### Suicide Coverage

**Recommendations (Based on Research)**
- Don't describe method in detail
- Don't glorify or romanticize
- Don't place prominently or use sensational headlines
- Include resources for help
- Focus on help-seeking and recovery when possible

### Tragedy and Trauma

- Approach victims and families with compassion
- Don't ambush grieving people
- Give people time before requesting interviews
- Accept "no" gracefully
- Consider the cumulative impact on affected communities

## Digital Ethics

### Social Media

- Same standards apply online as in print
- Verify social media information independently
- Consider consent when using user content
- Be transparent about your identity online
- Separate personal opinions from professional work

### User-Generated Content

- Verify authenticity
- Get permission when possible
- Credit sources appropriately
- Be cautious with sensitive content

### Corrections Online

- Note significant corrections clearly
- Consider whether updates should be labeled
- Maintain record of changes
- Don't delete errors—correct them

### Comment Sections

- Monitor for misinformation
- Remove harmful content
- Don't let comments platform harassment

## Decision Framework

### When Facing Ethical Dilemmas

1. **What is the public interest?** Does the public need this information?

2. **What is the potential harm?** Who could be hurt? How severely?

3. **Are there alternatives?** Can the story be told without the harmful element?

4. **What are your motives?** Are you serving the public or seeking attention?

5. **What would your peers say?** Would other ethical journalists make the same choice?

6. **Can you defend it?** Could you explain this decision to the public?

### Questions to Ask

- Is this true?
- Is this fair?
- Is this necessary?
- Is this the right time?
- Have I considered all stakeholders?
- Would I be comfortable if this decision were made public?

## Accountability Checklist

### Before Publication

- [ ] All facts verified
- [ ] Sources identified or properly described
- [ ] Subject given opportunity to respond
- [ ] Conflicts disclosed
- [ ] Potential harms considered
- [ ] Privacy weighed against public interest
- [ ] Methods ethical and transparent

### After Publication

- [ ] Process for corrections in place
- [ ] Monitoring for errors
- [ ] Responding to legitimate concerns
- [ ] Standing by good work under pressure
- [ ] Learning from mistakes

## Key Reminders

> "Journalism's first obligation is to the truth."
> — Bill Kovach and Tom Rosenstiel

> "The public has a right to know, but the journalist has a responsibility to be fair."
> — Traditional journalism principle

> "When in doubt, leave it out—unless the public interest clearly requires inclusion."
> — Ethical decision-making guideline

The goal is not perfection but a consistent commitment to ethical practice, willingness to admit mistakes, and ongoing learning.
