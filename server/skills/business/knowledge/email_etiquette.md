# Professional Email Etiquette Guide

Comprehensive guide to writing effective, professional business emails.

---

## Core Principles

### The 3 Cs of Business Email

1. **Clear**: State your purpose immediately
2. **Concise**: Respect the reader's time
3. **Courteous**: Maintain professional tone

### Before You Write

Ask yourself:
- Is email the right medium for this?
- Who really needs to receive this?
- What action do I need from the reader?
- What's the appropriate tone?

## Email Structure

### Subject Line

**Purpose**: Enable recipients to prioritize and find emails

**Best Practices**:
- Be specific: "Budget Approval Needed - Q3 Marketing" not "Question"
- Include action type: "FYI:", "Action Required:", "Decision Needed:"
- Add deadline if urgent: "Response Needed by Friday"
- Keep under 50 characters for mobile
- Update subject when topic changes

**Subject Line Formulas**:
| Type | Formula | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| Action | [Action]: [Topic] - [Deadline] | Action: Review Contract - Due Friday |
| Request | Request: [What] | Request: Interview Time Slots |
| Update | Update: [Topic] - [Status] | Update: Project X - On Track |
| FYI | FYI: [Topic] | FYI: New Office Hours |

### Greeting

**Formal** (external, senior leadership, first contact):
- Dear Mr./Ms. [Last Name],
- Dear Dr. [Last Name],
- Dear [Full Name],

**Professional** (colleagues, established relationships):
- Hi [First Name],
- Hello [First Name],
- Good morning/afternoon [First Name],

**Group**:
- Hi Team,
- Hi Everyone,
- Dear Colleagues,

### Opening Line

**Start Strong** - State your purpose in the first sentence:

| Purpose | Opening |
|---------|---------|
| Request | "I'm writing to request..." |
| Follow-up | "I'm following up on..." |
| Information | "I wanted to let you know..." |
| Response | "Thank you for your email about..." |
| Introduction | "I'm reaching out because..." |
| Bad news | "I'm writing to inform you that..." |

**Avoid**:
- "I hope this email finds you well" (in every email)
- "Sorry to bother you but..."
- "Just checking in..." (be specific)

### Body

**Structure for Clarity**:
1. Purpose (first sentence)
2. Context (only what's needed)
3. Details (organized logically)
4. Action/Request (specific)
5. Deadline (if applicable)

**Formatting Tips**:
- One idea per paragraph
- Use bullet points for lists
- Bold key information (sparingly)
- Keep paragraphs to 3-4 lines
- Use white space

### Closing

**Action-Oriented Closings**:
- "Please let me know by [date]"
- "I'd appreciate your response by [date]"
- "Please confirm receipt"
- "Let me know if you have questions"

**Relationship Closings**:
- "Thank you for your help"
- "I appreciate your time"
- "Looking forward to hearing from you"

### Sign-Off

**Formal**:
- Sincerely,
- Best regards,
- Respectfully,

**Professional**:
- Best,
- Thanks,
- Regards,

**Warm** (established relationships):
- Cheers,
- Talk soon,

## Tone and Language

### Professional Tone Spectrum

| Too Casual | Professional | Too Formal |
|------------|--------------|------------|
| "Hey!" | "Hi Sarah," | "Dear Ms. Johnson," (for colleague) |
| "gonna" | "going to" | "shall proceed to" |
| "ASAP!!!" | "by Friday" | "at your earliest convenience" |
| "np" | "You're welcome" | "It would be my pleasure" |

### Words to Use

| Instead of | Use |
|------------|-----|
| ASAP | Specific date/time |
| Per my last email | As I mentioned |
| Going forward | From now on / In the future |
| Touch base | Connect / Discuss |
| Circle back | Follow up |
| Deep dive | Detailed review |
| Bandwidth | Capacity / Time |

### Words to Avoid

| Avoid | Why |
|-------|-----|
| "Just" | Undermines your message |
| "Sorry to bother you" | Unnecessary apology |
| "To be honest" | Implies you're not usually honest |
| "Obviously" | Can seem condescending |
| "I think/feel" | Weakens professional statements |
| "You failed to" | Accusatory |

### Positive Framing

| Negative | Positive |
|----------|----------|
| "You didn't include..." | "Could you please add..." |
| "We can't do that" | "Here's what we can do" |
| "That's wrong" | "I see it differently" |
| "You need to" | "Would you be able to" |
| "I don't have time" | "My current workload won't allow" |

## Email Etiquette Rules

### Timing

**Sending**:
- Business hours when possible
- Respect time zones
- Use delay send for non-urgent off-hours emails
- Avoid Monday mornings and Friday afternoons for big requests

**Responding**:
- Acknowledge within 24 hours
- If you can't respond fully, send acknowledgment with timeline
- Set out-of-office for vacations

### CC and BCC

**CC (Carbon Copy)**:
- People who need to be informed but don't need to act
- Manager if they should be aware
- Relevant stakeholders for visibility

**BCC (Blind Carbon Copy)**:
- Large distribution lists (to protect privacy)
- Introducing someone then removing yourself
- Never use to secretly copy someone

**Reply vs Reply All**:
- Reply All: Only when everyone needs to see
- Reply: When only sender needs response
- Consider: "Does everyone need this?"

### Attachments

- Mention attachments in the body
- Use clear file names
- Compress large files
- Consider sharing links instead of large files
- Double-check you attached what you mentioned

### Email Chains

- Keep relevant history for context
- Summarize if chain is very long
- Start new thread when topic changes significantly
- Update subject line when direction changes

## Special Situations

### Sensitive Topics

- Consider if email is the right medium
- Phone or in-person may be better
- Avoid emotional language
- State facts objectively
- Offer to discuss in person

### Delivering Bad News

1. Be direct but compassionate
2. Explain the situation clearly
3. Take responsibility if appropriate
4. Provide next steps or alternatives
5. Offer support

### Urgent Requests

- Use "Urgent" sparingly (save for truly urgent)
- Explain why it's urgent
- Provide specific deadline with reason
- Consider calling instead
- Follow up with email for documentation

### Apologizing

**Structure**:
1. Acknowledge what happened
2. Take responsibility
3. Explain (don't make excuses)
4. State corrective action
5. Commit to prevention

**Example**:
"I apologize for sending the report with incorrect figures. I should have verified the data before sending. I've corrected the errors and attached the updated version. Going forward, I'll implement a verification checklist."

## Cultural Considerations

### General Guidelines

- Research cultural norms for international contacts
- When in doubt, err on formal side
- Be aware of holidays in other countries
- Consider language barriers
- Allow more time for responses

### Regional Preferences

| Region | Tendency |
|--------|----------|
| US | Direct, informal |
| UK | Polite, slightly formal |
| Germany | Very direct, formal |
| Japan | Hierarchical, formal |
| Latin America | Relationship-focused, warm |

## Email Don'ts

### Never

- Send when angry (draft, wait, revise)
- Put sensitive information in email
- Assume email is private
- Use ALL CAPS (reads as shouting)
- Overuse exclamation points!!!
- Use emoji in formal contexts
- Forward without permission
- Ignore emails

### Red Flags

- Excessive CC'ing
- Passive-aggressive tone
- Vague subject lines
- Wall of text
- "Reply All" abuse
- Missing greeting/closing
