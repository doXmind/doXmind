# Research Methods Reference Guide

## Qualitative Research Methods

### 1. Interviews

**Types:**
- **Structured**: Pre-determined questions, same order
- **Semi-structured**: Key questions with flexibility
- **Unstructured**: Conversational, topic-guided

**Best For:**
- Exploring experiences and perceptions
- Understanding motivations and attitudes
- Generating rich, detailed data

**Considerations:**
- Time-intensive
- Requires skilled interviewer
- Potential for interviewer bias

### 2. Focus Groups

**Characteristics:**
- 6-12 participants typically
- Moderated discussion
- Group dynamics can stimulate insights

**Best For:**
- Exploring shared perspectives
- Generating ideas
- Understanding social norms

### 3. Ethnography

**Characteristics:**
- Immersive observation
- Extended time in the field
- Understanding culture and context

**Best For:**
- Studying behavior in natural settings
- Understanding cultural practices
- Exploring complex social phenomena

### 4. Case Study

**Types:**
- Intrinsic: Study the case itself
- Instrumental: Use case to understand broader issue
- Collective: Multiple cases

**Best For:**
- In-depth exploration of phenomenon
- Complex, context-dependent situations
- Generating hypotheses

### 5. Grounded Theory

**Characteristics:**
- Theory emerges from data
- Iterative coding process
- Constant comparison

**Best For:**
- Developing new theory
- Exploring under-researched areas
- Understanding processes

## Quantitative Research Methods

### 1. Surveys

**Types:**
- Cross-sectional: One point in time
- Longitudinal: Over time
- Panel: Same participants over time

**Best For:**
- Large sample sizes
- Measurable variables
- Generalizable findings

### 2. Experiments

**Types:**
- True experiment (RCT): Random assignment
- Quasi-experiment: No randomization
- Natural experiment: Natural variation

**Best For:**
- Establishing causality
- Testing interventions
- Controlling variables

### 3. Correlational Studies

**Characteristics:**
- Examines relationships
- No manipulation of variables
- Cannot establish causation

**Best For:**
- When experiments aren't ethical/feasible
- Exploring associations
- Predictive models

## Mixed Methods Designs

### Sequential Explanatory
1. Quantitative data collection
2. Analysis
3. Qualitative follow-up to explain results

### Sequential Exploratory
1. Qualitative exploration
2. Analysis
3. Quantitative testing of themes

### Convergent Parallel
- Quantitative and qualitative collected simultaneously
- Results compared and integrated

### Embedded Design
- One method embedded within another
- Secondary method supports primary

## Sampling Methods

### Probability Sampling

| Method | Description | Use When |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| Simple Random | Every member has equal chance | Homogeneous population |
| Stratified | Divide into groups, random within | Distinct subgroups exist |
| Cluster | Randomly select groups | Geographically dispersed |
| Systematic | Select every nth member | Large, ordered population |

### Non-Probability Sampling

| Method | Description | Use When |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| Convenience | Easily accessible participants | Exploratory research |
| Purposive | Select based on criteria | Specific characteristics needed |
| Snowball | Participants recruit others | Hard-to-reach populations |
| Quota | Match population proportions | Known characteristics needed |

## Validity and Reliability

### Validity Types
- **Internal**: Cause-effect relationship accurate
- **External**: Results generalizable
- **Construct**: Measuring what intended
- **Face**: Appears to measure construct

### Reliability Types
- **Test-retest**: Consistent over time
- **Inter-rater**: Agreement between observers
- **Internal consistency**: Items measure same construct (Cronbach's α)

### Threats to Validity
- Selection bias
- History effects
- Maturation
- Testing effects
- Instrumentation changes
- Regression to mean
- Attrition

## Ethical Principles

### Belmont Report Principles
1. **Respect for Persons**: Autonomy, informed consent
2. **Beneficence**: Maximize benefits, minimize harm
3. **Justice**: Fair distribution of benefits/burdens

### Key Requirements
- Informed consent
- Voluntary participation
- Right to withdraw
- Confidentiality
- Data protection
- IRB/Ethics approval
