# Voice and Tone Guide

Creating consistent personality in product copy.

---

## Voice vs. Tone

### Voice = Personality (Constant)

Your product's voice is its character—consistent across all touchpoints.
- Who are you?
- What do you stand for?
- How do you sound in every interaction?

### Tone = Mood (Variable)

Tone adapts to context while voice stays the same.
- How should we sound right now?
- What's the user feeling?
- What's appropriate for this situation?

**Analogy**: Your voice is like your personality—it doesn't change. Your tone is like your mood—it shifts based on circumstances.

## Defining Your Voice

### Step 1: Identify Voice Attributes

Choose 3-5 adjectives that describe your product's personality:

**Example Attributes**:
| Attribute | What It Means | What It's Not |
|-----------|---------------|---------------|
| Friendly | Approachable, warm | Overly casual, immature |
| Expert | Knowledgeable, confident | Arrogant, condescending |
| Clear | Simple, direct | Blunt, cold |
| Helpful | Supportive, proactive | Pushy, overbearing |

### Step 2: Create Voice Spectrum

For each attribute, define the boundaries:

**Friendly**
```
Too little ←——————|——————→ Too much
Cold, distant      Right       Unprofessional, immature
```

**Example in Writing**:
- Too little: "Your request has been processed."
- Just right: "Got it! We've sent your request."
- Too much: "OMG yay!!! We totally got your request! 🎉🎉🎉"

### Step 3: Define "This, Not That"

| We Are | We're Not |
|--------|-----------|
| Friendly | Casual/unprofessional |
| Expert | Know-it-all |
| Encouraging | Patronizing |
| Direct | Blunt or rude |
| Conversational | Slangy |

## Tone Guidelines by Context

### Success States

**User Feeling**: Relieved, accomplished
**Tone**: Celebratory, positive, brief

**Examples**:
- ✓ "Done! Your changes are saved."
- ✓ "Success! Welcome to the team."
- ✗ "Transaction completed successfully." (too formal)

---

### Error States

**User Feeling**: Frustrated, confused, anxious
**Tone**: Calm, helpful, apologetic (if our fault)

**Examples**:
- ✓ "Something went wrong. Let's try that again."
- ✓ "We couldn't save your changes. Check your connection."
- ✗ "Error. Invalid operation." (cold, unhelpful)
- ✗ "Oops! You broke something!" (blaming, too casual)

---

### Warning States

**User Feeling**: Uncertain, cautious
**Tone**: Clear, serious, informative

**Examples**:
- ✓ "This action can't be undone."
- ✓ "You're about to delete all your data."
- ✗ "Warning! Danger zone!" (alarming, vague)

---

### Onboarding

**User Feeling**: Curious, impatient, overwhelmed
**Tone**: Welcoming, encouraging, efficient

**Examples**:
- ✓ "Welcome! Let's get you set up in 2 minutes."
- ✓ "Nice work! You've completed the first step."
- ✗ "WELCOME!!!" (too enthusiastic)
- ✗ "Complete the following mandatory fields." (cold)

---

### Empty States

**User Feeling**: Confused, wondering what to do
**Tone**: Encouraging, helpful, guiding

**Examples**:
- ✓ "No projects yet. Create your first one!"
- ✓ "Your inbox is empty. That's a good thing!"
- ✗ "No data available." (unhelpful)

---

### Form Interactions

**User Feeling**: Task-focused, may be impatient
**Tone**: Clear, helpful, unobtrusive

**Examples**:
- ✓ "Enter your email" (field label)
- ✓ "8+ characters, one number" (password hint)
- ✗ "Please kindly enter your electronic mail address" (too wordy)

---

### Support/Help

**User Feeling**: Stuck, frustrated, needing assistance
**Tone**: Empathetic, patient, thorough

**Examples**:
- ✓ "We're here to help."
- ✓ "Still stuck? Contact our team."
- ✗ "Read the documentation." (dismissive)

## Testing Your Voice

### The Five-Second Test

Show users a piece of copy for 5 seconds. Ask:
- How did this make you feel?
- What three words would you use to describe the personality?
- Was anything confusing?

### The Consistency Test

Gather copy from different parts of your product. Do they sound like they're from the same source?

### The Competitor Test

Remove brand names. Can users identify which copy is yours vs. competitors?

## Common Voice Mistakes

### Inconsistency

**Problem**: Different screens sound like different products

**Fix**: Document voice, train writers, audit regularly

### Forcing Personality

**Problem**: Trying to be "fun" in serious contexts

**Fix**: Let tone adapt; voice doesn't mean every message is playful

### Being Too Generic

**Problem**: Copy could belong to any product

**Fix**: Infuse specific personality, use distinctive phrases

### Over-Casualness

**Problem**: Unprofessional when trust matters

**Fix**: Match tone to context; serious moments need serious tone

## Voice Examples by Company Type

### B2B SaaS

**Voice**: Expert, efficient, trustworthy
**Example**: "Your data is synced and secure."

### Consumer App

**Voice**: Friendly, encouraging, simple
**Example**: "Nice! You're on a 5-day streak."

### Financial Product

**Voice**: Trustworthy, clear, reassuring
**Example**: "Your payment is protected and encrypted."

### Health/Wellness

**Voice**: Supportive, calm, empowering
**Example**: "You're making great progress."

### Developer Tool

**Voice**: Technical, direct, respectful
**Example**: "Deployment complete. View logs →"
