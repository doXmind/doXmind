# The Art of Revision: From First Draft to Final Polish

Every great piece of writing is rewritten, not written. The distance between a rough first draft and a polished final piece is bridged by revision---a deliberate, multi-layered process that transforms raw ideas into clear, compelling prose. doXmind gives you a powerful set of tools designed specifically for this journey, from broad structural edits down to fine-grained word choices.

This guide walks you through a proven revision workflow, showing you how to use doXmind's diff review, version history, and targeted AI edits to take any draft from "good enough" to genuinely great.

## Why Revision Matters More Than You Think

Most writers spend the majority of their time on the first draft and rush through editing. Experienced writers flip that ratio. A first draft captures your ideas; revision shapes them into something a reader can follow, enjoy, and remember.

Revision is not just about fixing typos. It operates on multiple levels:

- **Structural revision** -- Does the argument flow logically? Are sections in the right order?
- **Paragraph-level revision** -- Does each paragraph have a clear purpose? Are transitions smooth?
- **Sentence-level revision** -- Is the prose clear and concise? Are there awkward constructions?
- **Surface-level editing** -- Grammar, spelling, punctuation, and formatting.

Trying to do all four at once is overwhelming. The key is to work from the top down, addressing big-picture issues before sweating the small stuff.

## A Four-Pass Revision Strategy

### Pass 1: Structural Review

Start by reading your entire draft without editing. Instead, take notes on the overall shape. Ask yourself:

- Does the opening hook the reader?
- Is there a clear thesis or central argument?
- Do sections build on each other in a logical sequence?
- Does the conclusion land with impact?

In doXmind, use the **outline view** in the sidebar to see your heading structure at a glance. If sections feel out of order, drag them into a better sequence. You can also ask doXmind AI to evaluate your structure:

> "Review the structure of this document. Are there any sections that should be reordered, merged, or split? Suggest a revised outline."

doXmind AI will analyze your headings, topic flow, and argument progression, then suggest specific reorganization moves.

### Pass 2: Paragraph-Level Editing

Now zoom in one level. Read each paragraph and ask:

- What is this paragraph's job? If you cannot state it in one sentence, the paragraph may be doing too much.
- Does the first sentence orient the reader?
- Does the last sentence transition smoothly to the next paragraph?

This is where doXmind's **quick edit actions** shine. Select a paragraph and use the inline AI menu to:

- **Condense** -- Tighten a wordy paragraph without losing meaning.
- **Expand** -- Add detail or examples where the writing feels thin.
- **Rewrite** -- Get a fresh take on a paragraph that is not working.

Each suggestion appears as a diff overlay, so you can compare the original and proposed text side by side before accepting or rejecting the change.

### Pass 3: Sentence-Level Polish

With the structure and paragraphs solid, turn your attention to individual sentences. Common issues to look for:

- **Passive voice** where active voice would be stronger.
- **Nominalizations** -- turning verbs into nouns ("make a decision" instead of "decide").
- **Filler words** -- "very," "really," "just," "actually," and "quite."
- **Overly long sentences** that lose the reader halfway through.

Select a section and ask doXmind AI for targeted help:

> "Edit this section for clarity and conciseness. Remove filler words, convert passive voice to active where appropriate, and shorten sentences that exceed 25 words."

The AI will produce a revised version with changes highlighted in doXmind's diff review panel. You retain full control -- accept individual changes, reject others, or modify the suggestion before applying it.

### Pass 4: Surface-Level Proofread

The final pass catches the small errors that undermine credibility. doXmind's built-in spellcheck highlights misspellings and common grammar issues in real time. For a deeper proofread, ask doXmind AI:

> "Proofread this document for grammar, spelling, punctuation, and consistency. Flag any issues without changing the meaning."

This is deliberately the last step. There is no point in perfecting the grammar of a paragraph you later delete during structural revision.

## Using Diff Review Effectively

doXmind's diff review is one of its most powerful revision features. Whenever doXmind AI suggests edits to your document, the changes appear as a visual diff -- deletions highlighted in red, additions in green -- directly in the editor.

Here is how to get the most from diff review:

### Review Changes in Context

Do not rush through accepting all changes at once. Read each change in the context of the surrounding text. A sentence that looks fine in isolation might clash with the paragraph's tone or repeat a point made earlier.

### Accept Selectively

You do not have to accept or reject an entire set of changes. Use the per-change accept/reject buttons to cherry-pick the edits that improve your writing while keeping passages you prefer in their original form.

### Iterate

If a suggested edit is close but not quite right, reject it and ask doXmind AI to try again with more specific instructions:

> "Rewrite this paragraph to be more concise, but keep the anecdote about the product launch."

Specificity in your prompts leads to better suggestions.

## Leveraging Version History

Revision sometimes means taking risks -- cutting a section, restructuring an argument, or rewriting an introduction from scratch. Version history gives you the safety net to be bold.

### How Version History Works

doXmind automatically saves snapshots of your document as you work. You can also create manual save points before making major changes. To access version history, open the document menu and select **Version History**.

From there, you can:

- **Browse previous versions** to see how your document has evolved.
- **Compare versions** side by side to see exactly what changed.
- **Restore a version** if a revision went in the wrong direction.

### Best Practices for Version History

1. **Name your save points.** Before a major revision pass, create a named version like "Pre-structure revision" or "Draft 2 -- before cutting section 4." This makes it easy to find the right version later.
2. **Do not be afraid to cut.** Knowing you can restore any previous version frees you to make aggressive edits. That paragraph you spent an hour writing? Cut it if it does not serve the piece. It is still safely in your history.
3. **Compare across passes.** After completing your revision, compare the first draft with the final version. Seeing the transformation is both satisfying and educational -- you will learn which revision moves consistently improve your writing.

## Targeted AI Edits for Specific Goals

Beyond general revision, doXmind AI excels at targeted edits for specific purposes:

### Adjusting Tone

> "Rewrite this section in a more conversational tone, as if explaining the concept to a colleague over coffee."

### Improving Readability

> "Simplify this passage to a 10th-grade reading level without losing technical accuracy."

### Strengthening Arguments

> "Identify the weakest argument in this section and suggest how to strengthen it with evidence or reasoning."

### Ensuring Consistency

> "Check this document for consistency in terminology, tense, and point of view. Flag any inconsistencies."

The key is to give doXmind AI a clear, specific task rather than a vague request like "make it better." The more precise your instruction, the more useful the result.

## Common Revision Pitfalls

Even with great tools, certain revision habits can hold you back:

- **Editing while writing.** Resist the urge to polish sentences during your first draft. Get the ideas down first; refine later.
- **Over-relying on AI suggestions.** doXmind AI is a powerful collaborator, but your judgment is the final authority. Not every suggestion will be right for your voice or audience.
- **Stopping too soon.** One pass is rarely enough. Professional writers routinely revise five, ten, or more times. Each pass catches things the previous one missed.
- **Ignoring feedback.** If you are writing for others, share your draft and incorporate their perspective. The document chat feature in doXmind makes it easy to gather feedback from collaborators.
