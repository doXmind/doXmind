# Quick Edit: One-Click Polish, Expand & Rewrite

Not every writing task needs a full conversation with the AI. Sometimes you just need to tighten a paragraph, simplify a sentence, or shift the tone of a section. That is exactly what Quick Edit is for.

Quick Edit gives you one-click access to doXmind AI's most common editing actions. Select text, choose an action, and see the result instantly -- complete with a diff view that shows you exactly what changed. Accept the edit, reject it, or tweak it further. No chat window required.

This guide covers every Quick Edit action, explains when to use each one, and shows before-and-after examples so you know exactly what to expect.

## How Quick Edit Works

The Quick Edit workflow is designed to be fast and frictionless.

1. **Select text.** Highlight a word, sentence, paragraph, or multiple paragraphs in the editor.
2. **Choose an action.** A floating toolbar appears with available Quick Edit options. Click the one you need.
3. **Review the diff.** doXmind AI generates the edited version and presents it as a visual diff overlaid on your document. Additions are highlighted in green, removals in red.
4. **Accept or reject.** Click to accept the changes and apply them to your document, or reject to keep the original text.

The entire process takes seconds. You stay in your editor, in your flow, and in control.

## Quick Edit Actions

### Polish

**What it does:** Improves clarity, readability, and flow while preserving your meaning and voice. It tightens loose phrasing, fixes awkward constructions, and smooths transitions.

**When to use it:** After you have a solid draft and want to elevate the prose quality. Polish is your go-to action for general improvement.

**Before:**
> The thing about writing documentation is that it is something that a lot of developers do not really enjoy doing very much, but it is actually really important for the success of the project in the long run.

**After:**
> Writing documentation is something most developers avoid, but it is one of the most important factors in a project's long-term success.

The meaning is identical. The sentence is half the length and twice as clear.

### Simplify

**What it does:** Reduces complexity. It breaks long sentences into shorter ones, replaces jargon with plain language, and strips unnecessary qualifiers.

**When to use it:** When your text is too dense, too technical for the audience, or when you catch yourself using five words where two would do.

**Before:**
> The implementation of the aforementioned architectural paradigm necessitates a comprehensive evaluation of the existing infrastructure components and their interoperability characteristics prior to the commencement of the migration process.

**After:**
> Before starting the migration, evaluate your existing infrastructure and check how well the components work together.

Simplify is ruthless about cutting complexity. Use it when your reader should not need a dictionary to understand your point.

### Expand

**What it does:** Takes a brief passage -- an outline point, a terse paragraph, a skeletal description -- and develops it into fuller, more detailed prose.

**When to use it:** When you have captured an idea in shorthand and need to flesh it out. Expand is particularly useful for turning bullet-point outlines into narrative paragraphs.

**Before:**
> Version control is important. Teams should use Git. Branching strategies matter.

**After:**
> Version control is the backbone of any collaborative software project. It provides a complete history of every change, makes it safe to experiment, and ensures that no work is ever truly lost. Git has become the industry standard for version control, and for good reason: it is fast, distributed, and supported by every major hosting platform. However, simply using Git is not enough. Teams need a clear branching strategy -- whether it is Git Flow, trunk-based development, or a custom approach -- to prevent merge conflicts and keep the codebase stable as multiple people contribute simultaneously.

Expand takes your seed idea and grows it into a full thought.

### Change Tone

**What it does:** Adjusts the register and voice of your text. You can shift between formal, casual, professional, friendly, academic, and other tones.

**When to use it:** When the content is right but the voice is wrong. Maybe you drafted something casually and need to present it to executives, or you wrote a stiff technical description that needs to feel approachable on a blog.

**Formal to Casual:**

**Before:**
> We are pleased to announce the availability of our latest product update, which incorporates numerous enhancements designed to improve user productivity and satisfaction.

**After:**
> Great news -- our latest update just dropped, and it is packed with improvements that will make your workflow faster and smoother.

**Casual to Professional:**

**Before:**
> So basically our app crashed a bunch of times last week and we finally figured out what went wrong.

**After:**
> Following a series of service interruptions last week, our engineering team has identified and resolved the root cause of the instability.

Same information, completely different impression.

### Fix Grammar

**What it does:** Corrects grammatical errors, spelling mistakes, punctuation issues, and subject-verb agreement problems without changing your style or meaning.

**When to use it:** As a final pass before publishing, or whenever you suspect something reads oddly but you cannot pinpoint the issue.

**Before:**
> Their going to release there new product, which effect alot of users who's been waiting for along time.

**After:**
> They are going to release their new product, which affects a lot of users who have been waiting for a long time.

Fix Grammar is surgical. It corrects errors and nothing else.

### Rewrite

**What it does:** Generates a completely new version of the selected text. The meaning stays the same, but the structure, phrasing, and approach may be entirely different.

**When to use it:** When you feel stuck on a passage. You have revised it three times and it still does not feel right. Rewrite gives you a fresh perspective -- a different way to say the same thing that might unlock the paragraph.

**Before:**
> Our platform uses machine learning algorithms to analyze user behavior and predict what content they will find most relevant. This helps us deliver personalized recommendations that keep users engaged.

**After:**
> Every time a user interacts with our platform, we learn something. Our machine learning system observes these patterns and builds an evolving picture of what each person finds valuable. The result is a recommendation engine that surfaces the right content at the right moment, turning casual visitors into engaged readers.

Rewrite does not just rearrange words. It reimagines the passage from a different angle.

### Make Shorter

**What it does:** Condenses the selected text, removing redundancy and tightening the prose while retaining all essential information.

**When to use it:** When you are over a word count, when a section feels bloated, or when you want to respect your reader's time.

**Before:**
> In order to be able to successfully and effectively complete the process of onboarding new team members, it is absolutely essential and critically important that you take the time to carefully prepare and organize all of the necessary documentation and training materials well in advance of their anticipated start date.

**After:**
> To onboard new team members successfully, prepare all documentation and training materials before their start date.

One sentence instead of one paragraph. Every word earns its place.

### Make Longer

**What it does:** Expands the selected text by adding detail, examples, supporting points, or richer description.

**When to use it:** When a passage is too sparse or when a reviewer has asked you to elaborate on a point.

**Before:**
> Code reviews catch bugs early.

**After:**
> Code reviews are one of the most effective ways to catch bugs before they reach production. When a second pair of eyes examines a pull request, they bring a fresh perspective that often spots issues the original author overlooked -- from logic errors and edge cases to security vulnerabilities and performance concerns. Beyond bug detection, code reviews also promote knowledge sharing, enforce coding standards, and create a culture of collective ownership over the codebase.

## Combining Quick Edits

Quick Edit actions are composable. You can chain them to achieve exactly the result you want.

For example, if you have a rough, casual draft of a technical concept:

1. **Expand** -- Flesh out the skeleton into full paragraphs.
2. **Change Tone** -- Shift from casual to professional.
3. **Polish** -- Smooth out the final result.

Or if you are cutting a long report down to a summary:

1. **Make Shorter** -- Cut the text down aggressively.
2. **Simplify** -- Ensure the condensed version is accessible.
3. **Fix Grammar** -- Clean up any issues introduced by the compression.

Each action builds on the last, and the diff view at each step ensures you stay in control.

## Tips for Getting the Best Results

- **Select the right scope.** Quick Edit works best on complete units of text -- a full sentence, a complete paragraph, or a whole section. Selecting partial sentences can lead to awkward results.
- **Use the diff view carefully.** Read through the changes before accepting. The AI's edit is a suggestion, not a command.
- **Try multiple actions.** If Polish does not quite hit the mark, try Rewrite for a completely fresh take.
- **Work section by section.** Edit one paragraph at a time rather than selecting your entire document. This gives you granular control.
