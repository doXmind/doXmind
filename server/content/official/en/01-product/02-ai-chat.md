# Mastering AI Chat: doXmind AI as Your Writing Partner

The AI chat panel is the most versatile feature in doXmind. It is where you have direct conversations with doXmind AI about your writing -- brainstorming ideas, restructuring arguments, refining tone, generating content, and working through the creative challenges that every writer faces.

Unlike a generic chatbot, doXmind AI is deeply integrated with your editor. It can see your document, understand your context, and apply changes directly to your text. This guide will show you how to use the chat panel effectively and turn doXmind AI into a genuine writing partner.

## Opening and Navigating the Chat Panel

The chat panel lives alongside your editor. Click the chat icon in the toolbar or use the keyboard shortcut to toggle it open. The panel slides in from the right, giving you a split view: your document on the left, your conversation on the right.

You can resize the panel by dragging its edge, and you can collapse it when you need full-screen focus on your writing. Your conversation history persists, so you can close the panel and return later without losing context.

### Starting a New Conversation

Each document can have its own chat thread. When you open the chat panel for a new document, you start with a fresh conversation. The AI automatically has access to your document content, so you do not need to paste text into the chat -- it already knows what you have written.

## How to Prompt Effectively

The quality of doXmind AI's responses depends heavily on how you frame your requests. Here are proven strategies for getting excellent results.

### Be Specific About What You Want

Vague prompts produce vague results. Instead of asking "Make this better," tell the AI exactly what you need.

**Weak prompt:**
> "Improve my introduction."

**Strong prompt:**
> "Rewrite my introduction to hook the reader with a surprising statistic about remote work. Keep it under 100 words and maintain a professional but approachable tone."

The strong prompt gives the AI four clear constraints: the hook type, the topic, the length, and the tone. This specificity leads to a response you can actually use.

### Provide Context and Intent

doXmind AI can see your document, but it cannot read your mind. When you ask for help, share the context that matters.

- **Audience:** "This is for senior engineering managers who are evaluating new tools."
- **Purpose:** "I need this section to convince skeptical readers that the migration is worth the effort."
- **Constraints:** "This needs to fit in a 5-minute presentation, so keep it concise."
- **Style reference:** "Match the tone of my previous section -- informal but data-driven."

### Use Role-Based Prompting

Asking doXmind AI to adopt a specific role can dramatically improve its output.

- "Act as a developmental editor and identify the three weakest arguments in my essay."
- "You are a technical writer. Rewrite this API documentation to be clearer for junior developers."
- "Pretend you are a skeptical reader. What questions would you have after reading this section?"

This technique works because it primes the AI to apply domain-specific judgment rather than generic writing advice.

### Iterate in Conversation

The chat panel is designed for back-and-forth dialogue. You do not need to get the perfect result on the first try. Start with a broad request, evaluate the response, and refine.

1. "Draft an outline for a blog post about sustainable software engineering."
2. "Good start. Expand section 3 -- I want more focus on energy-efficient algorithms."
3. "Now write the introduction based on this outline. Open with a provocative question."
4. "Too formal. Make it sound like I am talking to a fellow developer over coffee."

Each message builds on the previous one, and doXmind AI maintains the full context of your conversation.

## Using Extended Thinking

For complex requests -- restructuring a long document, developing a nuanced argument, or analyzing the logical flow of an essay -- you can enable extended thinking mode. This gives doXmind AI more time and computational depth to reason through your request before responding.

Extended thinking is particularly useful for:

- **Structural analysis:** "Review my entire document and suggest a better organization for the sections."
- **Argument evaluation:** "Identify logical gaps or unsupported claims in my essay."
- **Complex rewrites:** "Rewrite this technical explanation for a non-technical audience while preserving all key information."
- **Strategic planning:** "I have 20 pages of research notes. Help me distill them into a 2,000-word article outline."

To enable extended thinking, toggle it on in the chat panel before sending your message. The AI will take a bit longer to respond, but the depth of analysis is significantly greater.

## Attaching Images

doXmind AI supports multimodal conversations. You can attach images directly in the chat panel -- up to 10 images per message. This opens up several powerful workflows.

### Visual Content Writing

If you are writing about a chart, diagram, or photograph, attach it and ask the AI to help you describe or analyze it.

- "Here is a screenshot of our Q3 dashboard. Write a summary paragraph highlighting the key trends."
- "I have attached a system architecture diagram. Help me write the technical description for our documentation."

### Reference and Inspiration

Attach images of layouts, designs, or examples you want to reference.

- "This is a screenshot of a competitor's landing page. Help me write copy that is more compelling."
- "Here is a photo from the event. Write a caption that captures the energy of the moment."

### Feedback on Visual Documents

If your writing includes visual elements, you can get the AI's perspective on how text and images work together.

- "I have attached the infographic that will accompany this section. Does my text complement the visual, or am I repeating what the image already shows?"

## Practical Chat Workflows

Here are some of the most valuable ways to use the chat panel in your daily writing.

### Brainstorming and Ideation

When you are staring at a blank page, the chat panel is the best place to start.

- "Give me 10 possible angles for a blog post about developer burnout."
- "I want to write about AI in education. What are the most under-discussed subtopics?"
- "Help me come up with a metaphor that explains microservices to a non-technical audience."

### Outline Development

Once you have an idea, use the chat to build structure before you write.

- "Create a detailed outline for this article. Include main points and supporting evidence for each section."
- "I have a rough outline in my document. Review it and suggest improvements to the flow."

### Drafting and Expansion

The AI can generate first drafts that you then refine in the editor.

- "Write a first draft of section 2 based on my outline. Aim for 300 words."
- "Expand this bullet list into full paragraphs. Maintain my existing voice."

### Revision and Critique

Use doXmind AI as an honest editor who is always available.

- "Read my conclusion and tell me if it effectively summarizes my main arguments."
- "What is the weakest paragraph in this essay? Explain why and suggest an improvement."
- "Check this section for jargon that a general audience would not understand."

### Research and Fact-Checking

While doXmind AI should not be your sole source of truth, it can help you organize and verify information.

- "What are the most commonly cited statistics about remote work productivity? I want to fact-check the numbers in my article."
- "Summarize the key arguments for and against this position so I can present a balanced view."

## Tips for Better Conversations

After working with thousands of writers, these patterns consistently produce the best results.

1. **One request per message.** Complex, multi-part prompts often lead to incomplete responses. Break them into separate messages.
2. **Quote specific text.** When referring to part of your document, quote it directly so the AI knows exactly what you mean.
3. **State your format preference.** If you want bullet points, a table, or prose, say so upfront.
4. **Push back.** If the AI's suggestion does not feel right, say why. "This is too generic" or "I need a more personal angle" gives it useful signal.
5. **Save good prompts.** When you find a prompt that works well, remember it. Great prompts are reusable across projects.
